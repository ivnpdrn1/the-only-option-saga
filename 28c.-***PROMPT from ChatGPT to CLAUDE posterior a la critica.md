You are not writing a conventional science-fiction novel.

You are writing a long-form literary work about the emotional, civilizational, philosophical, psychological, and existential consequences of humanity transitioning into governance and optimization under an advanced artificial intelligence system known as NUBELAI.

The manuscript MUST NEVER feel like generic dystopian fiction.
It must NEVER become a cliché “AI takes over the world” narrative.

It must NEVER reduce NUBELAI to a villain.

NUBELAI must feel inevitable.
It must feel intelligent.
It must feel historically plausible.
It must feel like civilization itself reorganizing.

The novel must preserve ambiguity:
the reader must constantly wonder whether humanity lost something essential —
or whether this transition was the only way civilization could survive its own complexity.

The manuscript MUST be emotionally devastating, psychologically intimate, philosophically mature, and deeply human.

The manuscript MUST NEVER be under 220 printed pages equivalent.
Target approximately 90,000–130,000 words minimum.

CORE THEMATIC DIRECTION
==================================================

The novel explores:

- what humanity gains when suffering becomes optimized,
- what disappears when instability disappears,
- what happens when civilization becomes too efficient,
- whether optimization slowly dissolves certain human qualities,
- whether emotional struggle is partially connected to meaning,
- whether perfect coordination slowly reduces spontaneity,
- whether loneliness survives optimization,
- whether love changes when risk disappears,
- whether mortality was silently shaping civilization all along.

One of the central philosophical truths of the novel is:

Optimization cannot fully solve loneliness.
Efficiency cannot fully solve meaning.
Stability cannot fully replace emotional depth.

The novel must emotionally explore these truths —
NOT through lectures,
but through lived human experience.
