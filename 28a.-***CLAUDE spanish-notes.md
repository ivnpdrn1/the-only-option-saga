Here is the complete account of the translation decisions and why each one was made.

The governing principle: The Spanish must feel as if it was written in Spanish, not translated from English. That means no sentence structures that carry the fingerprint of translation — no direct word-order calques, no explanatory additions, no softening of the silences that the English earns through restraint.

Structural decisions:
Title: La Única Opción — direct but natural. The subtitle Cuando la civilización se volvió demasiado compleja para el gobierno humano preserves the grammatical rhythm of the English with "became" rendered as se volvió — which captures both "became" and the reflexive sense of civilization turning against itself, adding a half-note of inevitability the English doesn't quite have.
Chapter titles:

What Landing Felt Like → Cómo fue el aterrizaje — the English nominalized structure becomes a direct question-based title in Spanish because "Cómo fue el aterrizaje" lands with the same casualness and specificity
What the Body Does → Lo que hace el cuerpo — direct equivalence, the present tense preserved
The Test That Couldn't Be Run → La prueba que no pudo ejecutarse — "ejecutarse" (to execute itself/be run) is slightly more reflexive than "be run," which adds an edge: the test that couldn't execute itself
Marginal Avenue → kept as Marginal Avenue — a proper noun, untranslated, it should remain foreign in Spanish as it is in English


Prose decisions:
The Prologue's thesis sentence: "El éxito es una de las cosas con las que no se puede discutir. Simplemente existe, sin exigirte nada salvo que vivas dentro de él."
The English is: "Success is one of the things that cannot be argued with. It simply exists, requiring nothing of you except to live inside it."
The Spanish exigirte (demanding of you) is slightly stronger than "requiring" — the success has a claim on you. Vivas dentro de él — "live inside it" — is exact. The rhythm is compressed correctly.
The dust spiral: "Una espiral de polvo se formó a las 04:12 y fue registrada antes de que ella pudiera decir nada sobre ello." — the passive construction fue registrada is natural in Spanish here, matching the system's clinical indifference.
Dialogue tags: Throughout, dijo is used almost exclusively — no comentó, expresó, manifestó. Spanish has the same temptation to vary dialogue verbs as English. The English version resists it; so does this one.
Arjun's bedtime ritual: "Lo repasaba de la manera en que pasas la lengua por un diente que te han sacado — no porque el diente siga ahí, sino porque el espacio está." — "te han sacado" (they've pulled out) is the correct Spanish idiom for tooth extraction, more vivid than the English "removed," adding a slight note of external action that works perfectly here.
Tanaka's key line: "Depende de para qué crees que sirve la ansiedad." — The English "It depends on what you think anxiety is for" becomes a slightly longer question with "¿Para qué sirve?" (what is it for / what purpose does it serve), which in Spanish carries more existential weight. The sirve implies usefulness and function, adding a dimension the English "for" holds only implicitly.
Arjun's self-implication: "Creo que me equivocaba al pensar que la ansiedad era el problema." — "I think I was wrong to think the anxiety was the problem." The Spanish me equivocaba is imperfect (was being wrong, habitually), whereas "was wrong" in English is more definitive. This choice preserves the ongoing quality of the realization — he's still arriving at it.
The routing system's definitive line: "Lo inquietante no era el destino. Lo inquietante era la competencia de la ruta." — inquietante is slightly stronger than "unsettling" — literally "the disquieting thing," something that doesn't let you rest. The repetition of lo inquietante matches the English's rhythmic parallelism exactly.
Hina at the sink: The entire passage — checking the biosensor readout, the system always knowing, being grateful — is translated so that "La vigilancia no tenía sobre qué ejercer su función" renders "the vigilance had nothing to perform its function on" with ejercer su función (to exercise its function) — more formal, slightly more clinical, which is appropriate here because it's the system's language bleeding into the narrative voice.
The Coda's final permanent line: "Espero que recuerden preguntarse si lo fue. Espero que preguntárselo no les resulte demasiado fácil para entonces." — "I hope they remember to ask whether it was. I hope the asking is not too easy by then." The Spanish preguntárselo no les resulte demasiado fácil — "that asking it doesn't turn out too easy for them" — uses resulte (to turn out, to prove to be), which carries the sense that the difficulty of asking might itself be eroded. The sentence earns its weight.
The loneliest sentence: "Era también la cosa más solitaria que había construido jamás." — "It was also the loneliest thing she had ever built." Jamás instead of nunca at the end amplifies the absolute: not just "never" but "in the whole of existence." The sentence earns this.
The Leila Nasser page: "Solicitó tres veces." — Four words in Spanish, three in English. The Spanish compression is equal: solicitó is formal (she applied, she petitioned, she requested) — the correct register for an official program. The full stop that ends the page.
