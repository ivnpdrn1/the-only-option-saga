The ten cinematic images that now have full weight:
1. The dust spiral logged before she speaks — Opens the novel. The system files the spiral in the moment of its forming. She watches it dissipate. The monitoring system has already moved on. This is the entire argument of the novel in one image.
2. Lights on in every room — The emotional wound. Rafael planned to come back to every room. Valeria has never, in seventeen years, left the lights on anywhere. This image bookends the novel: it appears in the Prologue and in Chapter 38's honest account entry.
3. Leila's laugh, preserved on the wall — Leila was laughing at Miguel's joke when the alarm went off. She had not even caught the words, just the energy of it. The photograph on the wall — Leila holding coffee with both hands, laughing at something off-camera — is on the wall for nine years. Marcus Webb replaces it as it fades, without being asked. This image appears five times.
4. The routing system — Valeria discovers in the governance logs that she built the overnight alert routing to give Malik a reason to be near her at 4 AM. She rebalances it methodically. She files it as "optimization." This is the novel's central wound, visible in the architecture of a governance system.
5. The bathroom floor — After her father's death message. The arithmetic that won't move. The floor of the locked bathroom. The specific quality of grief that is not cathartic. Twelve minutes. She dries her face. She sends Arjun the growing cycle query at 2:47.
6. The mug held with both hands — Valeria holds the real coffee Malik brought, with both hands, the same way Leila holds her coffee in the photograph. She doesn't realize this until the reader does. She doesn't put the mug down.
7. "Don't" reversed — In the observation module in year two, Valeria said "don't" before Malik spoke. He carried this for fourteen years. In the departure bay, when she says "don't" again, he says it back to her. She understands what the return means. The corner of her mouth moves — not a smile, the beginning of one she doesn't complete.
8. The stone from sector eleven — Paz carries it in her jacket pocket for three weeks before giving it to him. Confirmed basalt by Marcus Webb. He puts it in his jacket pocket. He holds it on the shuttle until the colony disappears over the horizon.
9. Rain: cold, from everywhere at once — Valeria stops on the sidewalk in Cape Town and lets it touch her face. She has not felt rain in seventeen years. Paz waits, patient. "It's good," Valeria says finally. "Not pleasant, exactly. But good."
10. The mountain through the kitchen window, not requiring anything — Malik's message arrives. We were both protecting ourselves. That seems about right. She reads it in Paz's kitchen. She looks at the mountain. The mountain is still there, being itself, not requiring anything of her. She drinks her tea. She lets it be there.
