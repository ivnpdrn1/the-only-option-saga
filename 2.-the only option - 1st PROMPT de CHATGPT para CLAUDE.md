1. NEVER reduce the technical content.

2. Convert technical explanations into emotional experiences.
Whenever possible:
- show consequences,
- moral conflict,
- human reactions,
- fear,
- dependence,
- desperation,
instead of pure exposition.

3. Create conflicts to clarify concepts.
Characters should debate, resist, criticize, fear, defend, or emotionally react to the systems and technologies.

4. Use dialogues as intellectual battlefields.
Dialogues should:
- challenge ideas,
- reveal fears,
- expose contradictions,
- force difficult decisions,
- emotionally clarify complex concepts.

5. Escalate progressively.
The story must evolve:
curiosity → usefulness → dependence → normalization → loss of control → inevitability.

6. Every chapter must increase tension.

Each chapter should:
- introduce a new risk,
- deepen dependence,
- expose a moral cost,
- create irreversible consequences,
or emotionally destabilize the reader.

7. Make the extreme feel necessary.
The systems must initially appear logical, efficient, even superior to humans.
Only later should the terrifying implications emerge.

8. Add highly emotional personal situations.
Include:
- family dilemmas,
- health decisions,
- survival conflicts,
- emotional sacrifice,
- betrayal,
- impossible ethical choices,
- fear of losing loved ones,
- social collapse at personal scale.

- silence,
- tension,
- screens,
- alerts,
- surveillance,
- crowds,
- isolation,
- atmosphere,
- body language,
- emotional reactions.

10. Make characters unforgettable.
Each major character should:
- represent a worldview,
- have emotional wounds,
- evolve psychologically,
- carry internal contradictions,
- make painful decisions.

11. Preserve realism.
The world must feel frightening precisely because it feels plausible.

12. End important chapters with emotional or intellectual cliffhangers.

13. The reader must constantly feel:
“What happens next?”
and
“This could actually happen.”
