The eight additions:
Chapter 9b — What the Sleep Data Said
Tanaka has been keeping a parallel record — not the official behavioral reports, but a private document tracking what the numbers don't fully hold. The sleep data improves dramatically with NUBELAI's integration: the average goes up, which is the news. What she notices and doesn't report is that the variance disappears. Everyone sleeping in a narrow band of adequacy, nightly, like a maintained system. Then a second metric: reflexive anxiety latency — how quickly people get scared. It increases year by year, not because people become less capable but because they learn, correctly, that their fear isn't needed. The system handles things before the fear is useful. Tanaka watches this without knowing whether it's adaptation or attenuation. She keeps the parallel file. This chapter establishes the psychological record underneath the colony's success, and introduces the distinction that will haunt every subsequent chapter: the difference between rational adaptation and the loss of something that was doing more than one thing.
Chapter 10b — What the First Year Produced
Three panels of Paz at 5, 9, and 15. At five: her cognitive absorption of the system as a fact rather than a choice — not frightening, not wondrous, just the conditions. At nine: watching Valeria after the competency drill, asking whether she misses what it felt like before, receiving the honest answer: "like something that was true about me that isn't true anymore, and I'm not sure yet whether that's an ending or a beginning." At fifteen: presenting to the Year Twelve cohort, answering "don't you feel like something was taken from you?" with precision — she can't feel nostalgic for the surgical anxiety of 1850, and she can't feel nostalgic for the pre-integration colony. What she doesn't know is whether something was lost. The three panels chart Mars-native cognition without sentimentalizing or pathologizing it. Paz is not damaged. She is genuinely different. The difference cannot be evaluated from inside it.
Chapter 15b — Geneva, Year Eight
Amara fourteen months after Copenhagen, managing the implementation of the frameworks she built. Two scenes. First: the moment she realizes that the governance architecture she created has become invisible — internalized, ambient, no longer discussed as a framework because it has become the background conditions. This is success. It also means the oversight processes are no longer conducted with the quality of attention that designed them. Second: her research assistant brings her a study showing near-miss reporting has declined in integrated environments — not because there are fewer near-misses but because the system catches them before professionals see them. The professionals are losing the expertise developed through repeated exposure to the texture of imminent problems. Amara and Valeria compare notes for the first time and discover they have been watching the same thing from different sides. Neither knows if their protocols are sufficient. Both keep the folders.
What the Hospitals Stopped Tracking
Two scenes of loss through success at intimate human scale. Dr. Tesfaye in Rotterdam watches pediatric emergency visits fall eighty-three percent. The clinical outcomes improve. The waiting rooms go quiet. His professional skills become more concentrated and high-stakes. What he can no longer practice is the ordinary cases that, through repetition, produced the intuition that caught the outliers in the ordinary cases. He retires early. He gives personal circumstances as the reason. The second scene: a São Paulo neonatal unit where nurses had an informal practice of standing at a new infant's bedside for five minutes after completing setup — not documented, transmitted only by witnessing. This produced embodied familiarity with each infant's specific quality of breathing, against which subtle early changes could be felt before the monitors registered them. The practice disappears within two years of integration. The monitors catch the changes too. The clinical outcomes remain excellent. The intuition retires.
A Glossary of Disappeared Verbs
Tanaka's private linguistic file: five entries about words and constructions that are changing or disappearing. To worry about — contracting away from infrastructure and into interpersonal domains, because infrastructure is systemsafe. To check — migrating to interpersonal verification; in the under-25 population, "check" now means checking on a friend, not checking the infrastructure. To keep watch — has become a historical verb, appearing in documentary contexts about pre-transition practices. To hedge — speakers in integrated environments are hedging less in their own speech, not because they're more confident, but because the synthesis provides the uncertainty quantification; someone is handling the epistemological humility. To sit with — contracting toward the human-domain problems, the ones with no synthesis output. Tanaka's final note: the phrase is becoming a signal that this is the kind of problem that has to be held by a person.
What the Architects Designed For
Carmen Fuentes designing a residential district in Concepción writes in her process journal: she keeps designing for failure and removing it, and the result is a space that makes her uncomfortable in a way she can't justify. Previous cities were built by people who were afraid of specific things — floods, fires, siege, infrastructure failure. You can read the fear in the design. The new cities will have no such reading. They will look like cities built by people who were not afraid. She doesn't know what that means. She wins three architecture awards. She never publishes the process journal entry.
Year Sixteen: What She Noticed About the Silence and What She Gave Paz
Two sections of Valeria's last full year. First: she notices that the specific atmospheric pressure of the colony — the quality of air in a building where someone could be injured if attention lapsed, the future pressing on the present — has simply dissipated. She cannot separate how much is the system and how much is her having changed inside the system. She does the year's last competency drill and watches Petra check three things where she would have checked two: different mechanism, same outcome, different terrain. She walks the perimeter at 6 AM in December, the only walk she has done since year three, finding the junction she relocated on her first day. She does not tell anyone. Second: she gives Paz access to the honest account. Paz reads it and identifies the routing system entry immediately. Their conversation about why it was documented as an optimization rather than the real thing. Paz's decision to write her own honest account — from inside what Valeria built. The moment where the generational inheritance of the record is established: Paz is going to write the thing that Valeria was unable to write from outside her own system.
What the Earth Felt Like
Paz's months in Cape Town at full resolution. The flight, the landscape's unoptimized geometry. The mountain's indifference. Standing in the rain for twenty-two minutes: the Atlantic cold that is not directed at you, the rain that will fall regardless, not being logged. The market: she expects to be interested in the inefficiency, but what actually draws her is the argument. The small frictions of people with different information and different preferences occupying the same space. She writes: what gets lost in the optimization is the negotiation. Not the arguments. The deeper thing underneath: the ongoing adjustment of people to each other's reality. Then the August call to Hina. Thirty-two minutes. Her mother not worried. The monitoring data providing the function that worry previously provided. Paz understands for the first time that the anxious attention of her mother checking the biosensor readout was a form of presence. A form of the relationship. The system is more reliable at that function. Something has also disappeared. She writes: there is a form of love that consists of being the source of someone's worry. Not because you want them to suffer. Because worry is one of the ways the body produces the state of caring.
